\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Experimental Protocol}
\label{sec:exp_protocol}

\paragraph{Dataset and split}
We evaluate on LineMod~\cite{hinterstoisser2012model} (13 objects, RGB-D, cluttered scenes) using the \emph{official} per-object split that comes with the dataset --- the same protocol used by DenseFusion~\cite{wang2019densefusion}. About 15\% of each object's frames are for training and the remaining 85\% for testing: 2{,}373 training and 13{,}407 test images in total. A validation set (357 images) is carved out of the training pool for model selection and early stopping. The test set is never used for any training or selection decision, and we use \textbf{no synthetic or rendered training images}: all learning happens on the 2{,}016 real frames. We check split integrity (no overlaps and correct per-object counts) programmatically at data-loading time.

\paragraph{Metric}
We report the standard ADD accuracy: a pose counts as correct if the average distance between model points transformed by the predicted and ground-truth poses is below 10\% of the object diameter. For the two symmetric objects (eggbox, glue) we use the closest-point variant ADD-S. Diameters come from the dataset's \texttt{models\_info} file; $N{=}500$ model points are used throughout.

\paragraph{Training}
All pose models are trained with Adam~\cite{kingma2014adam} (lr $10^{-4}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$), batch size 64, ADD loss (Eq.~\ref{eq:add_loss}), \texttt{ReduceLROnPlateau} scheduling, and early stopping on validation ADD (patience 30, epoch cap 400). Only photometric augmentation is used (Sec.~\ref{sec:method_fusion}).

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Object Detection}
\label{sec:exp_detection}

YOLO11n is fine-tuned for 100 epochs on the official training frames only. On the 13{,}407 test images it achieves 99.5 mAP@50 and 94.2 mAP@50-95, with 99.98 precision and 99.99 recall, and finds a usable box for every frame (zero detection misses at confidence 0.25): this means the downstream pose evaluation can run fully end-to-end. Detection on LineMod works reliably even with limited training data; this is important for our pipeline because the bounding box is also used to compute the geometric anchor (Sec.~\ref{sec:method_anchor}).

\subsection{RGB-only Baseline}
\label{sec:exp_rgb_baseline}

Table~\ref{tab:phase3} separates rotation from translation for the RGB-only baseline by swapping the translation source while keeping the predicted rotation fixed. Rotation is learnable from RGB crops: with an oracle translation the baseline reaches 76.7\% ADD(-S), and the score is the same whether the crop comes from ground-truth or predicted boxes --- more evidence that detection is not a bottleneck. Translation, on the other hand, does not work: the pinhole approximation drops to 7.5\% (because its spherical-object assumption fails on elongated shapes), and direct regression from RGB features to just 1.1\%. The anchored fusion model of Sec.~\ref{sec:method_anchor} addresses this failure.

\subsection{Ablation of the Translation Formulation}
\label{sec:exp_ablation}

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} isolates the effect of the anchored residual formulation (Sec.~\ref{sec:method_anchor}) by keeping the architecture ($\sim$36M parameters), training data, and recipe fixed, and only changing the geometry input and the regression target. Two learning-free references anchor the table: copying the per-object \emph{mean training pose} scores 0.4\%, and combining the anchor with the mean training rotation scores 10.4\% --- the score you can get from the geometric prior alone, with zero learning.

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figure/v2/ablation_bars.png}
    \caption{Visual summary of Table~\ref{tab:ablation}: with model, data, and recipe fixed, anchoring the translation target moves the same network from below the learning-free reference to above the DenseFusion per-pixel baseline.}

Two key observations. First, direct absolute regression (6.8\%) performs \emph{worse} than the learning-free anchor reference (10.4\%): the network cannot even recover the information that the depth median already provides, which confirms that the problem is in how the target is formulated rather than in the amount of data. Second, anchoring the target turns the same model into an accurate estimator (95.2\%), with the mean error dropping from 69.7 to 9.1\,mm. Across three independent training seeds, the \texttt{norm} variant scores $94.9 \pm 0.2$\%, showing that the result is not seed-dependent.

\subsection{Memorization Analysis}
\label{sec:exp_memorization}

Since LineMod sequences are densely sampled videos, a high accuracy could in theory just reflect the model retrieving similar training views rather than actually estimating the pose. We test this hypothesis directly with three measurements.

\paragraph{Nearest-neighbor bounds}
For each test frame we find the most visually similar training frame of the same object ($32{\times}32$ grayscale crop, cosine similarity) and copy its pose. This \emph{retrieval NN} baseline scores \textbf{2.3\%} ADD(-S). We also compute an \emph{oracle NN}, which copies the pose of the training frame closest to the test frame in pose space: this is the ceiling of \emph{any} strategy that answers with a memorized training pose, no matter how the retrieval works. The oracle scores \textbf{7.1\%}, with the symmetric ADD-S classes accounting for most of it. This rules out a pose-copying explanation for our 95.1\% result.

\paragraph{Why memorization fails here}
The official split leaves big viewpoint gaps: the median angular distance between a test frame and its nearest training view is $7.4^\circ$ (90th percentile: $10.5^\circ$). At these gaps, both the viewpoint and the camera-frame translation change enough that a copied pose misses the 10\%-diameter threshold almost everywhere.

\paragraph{Accuracy vs.\ viewpoint novelty}
Figure~\ref{fig:gapcurve} bins the end-to-end accuracy of our model by the angular distance to the nearest training view. The profile is flat --- 94--97\% for all bins up to $12^\circ$, which cover 96.4\% of the test set --- followed by a gradual drop in the extrapolation tail (85--86\% at $12$--$14^\circ$; 67--72\% beyond $14^\circ$, 327 samples). This profile is the same across all three training seeds. A model that just memorizes would degrade sharply even at small angular distances; what we see instead is consistent with a model that interpolates between viewpoints, and only degrades where real extrapolation is needed.
