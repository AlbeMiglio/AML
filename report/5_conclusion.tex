\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a modular pipeline for 6D object pose estimation on the official LineMod protocol, trained only on the $\sim$15\% real-image split. Our main finding is that, when training data is this limited, the most important design choice for a lightweight global-fusion architecture is \emph{how the translation target is defined}: direct absolute regression from pooled features fails (6.8\% ADD(-S), worse than what the depth median alone gives), while anchoring the prediction to a 3D point back-projected from the observed depth brings the same 36M-parameter model to 95.1\% ADD(-S) in a full end-to-end evaluation with predicted detections --- beating the per-pixel variant of DenseFusion under the same protocol and data conditions, with no per-point fusion, no refinement, and no synthetic training data. A memorization analysis (retrieval and oracle nearest-neighbor bounds at 2.3\% and 7.1\%, and a flat accuracy profile up to $12^\circ$ of viewpoint gap) supports the conclusion that the model is actually generalizing geometrically rather than memorizing. In this regime, expressing the regression target relative to observed geometry turns out to matter more than model capacity.

\paragraph{Limitations and Future Work}
We identify four main limitations. \textbf{(i) Small objects}: the two smallest classes (ape, duck) remain the weakest (87--90\%), because the diameter-relative ADD threshold shrinks to 10--11\,mm; sub-pixel refinements of the crop or a local per-pixel branch could help close this gap. \textbf{(ii) Extrapolation tail}: beyond $14^\circ$ from the nearest training view (2.4\% of the test set) accuracy drops to 67--72\%, marking the limit of viewpoint interpolation; synthetic view augmentation~\cite{he2020pvn3d, he2021ffb6d} is the standard remedy and is fully compatible with our approach. \textbf{(iii) Single anchor under occlusion}: the median anchor summarizes the visible surface with a single point. It handles detector jitter well (Sec.~\ref{sec:exp_results}), but we cannot assess how it behaves under heavy occlusion on this benchmark. Measuring visibility as the fraction of projected model points that fall inside the object mask, the official LineMod test split is almost occlusion-free: median visibility 0.95, 1st percentile 0.89, and a single frame below 0.85 in a 3{,}250-frame sample. Whether a single anchor breaks down where dense per-point methods succeed is therefore an open question, to be tested on Occlusion-LINEMOD; a sparse multi-anchor variant is the natural extension to try alongside it. \textbf{(iv) Aspect-ratio distortion}: the square crop with side $\max(w,h)$ resized to $224{\times}224$ introduces non-isotropic resampling; square padding would preserve the aspect ratio at negligible cost.
