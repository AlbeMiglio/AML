\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a modular pipeline for 6D object pose estimation on the official LineMod protocol, trained exclusively on the $\sim$15\% real-image split. Our main finding is that, in this low-data regime, the most important design choice for a lightweight global-fusion architecture is the \emph{formulation of the translation target}: direct absolute regression from pooled features fails (6.8\% ADD(-S), below what the depth median alone provides), while anchoring the prediction to a 3D point back-projected from the observed depth brings the same 36M-parameter model to 95.1\% ADD(-S) in a fully end-to-end evaluation with predicted detections --- above the per-pixel variant of DenseFusion under the same protocol and data regime, with no per-point fusion, no refinement, and no synthetic training data. A memorization analysis (retrieval and oracle nearest-neighbor bounds at 2.3\% and 7.1\%, and a flat accuracy profile up to $12^\circ$ of viewpoint novelty) supports the conclusion that the result reflects actual geometric generalization. In this regime, expressing the regression target relative to observed geometry appears to matter more than model capacity or input encoding.

\paragraph{Limitations and Future Work}
We identify four main limitations. \textbf{(i) Small objects}: the two smallest classes (ape, duck) remain the weakest (87--90\%), a consequence of the diameter-relative ADD threshold shrinking to 10--11\,mm; sub-pixel refinements of the crop or a local per-pixel branch could close this gap. \textbf{(ii) Extrapolation tail}: beyond $14^\circ$ from the nearest training view (2.4\% of the test set) accuracy drops to 67--72\%, marking the boundary of viewpoint interpolation; synthetic view augmentation~\cite{he2020pvn3d, he2021ffb6d} is the established remedy and is fully compatible with our formulation. \textbf{(iii) Single anchor under occlusion}: the median anchor summarizes the visible surface with a single point. It is robust to detector jitter (Sec.~\ref{sec:exp_results}), but its behaviour under heavy occlusion cannot be assessed on this benchmark. Measuring visibility as the fraction of projected model points that fall inside the object mask, the official LineMod test split is almost occlusion-free: median visibility 0.95, 1st percentile 0.89, and a single frame below 0.85 in a 3{,}250-frame sample. Whether a single anchor degrades where dense per-point methods hold up is therefore an open question, to be answered on Occlusion-LINEMOD; a sparse multi-anchor variant is the natural extension to test alongside it. \textbf{(iv) Aspect-ratio distortion}: the square crop with side $\max(w,h)$ resized to $224{\times}224$ introduces non-isotropic resampling; square padding would preserve the aspect ratio at negligible cost.
