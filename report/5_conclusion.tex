\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a modular pipeline for 6D object pose estimation on the official LineMod protocol, trained exclusively on the $\sim$15\% real-image split. Our main finding is that, in this low-data regime, the most important design choice for a lightweight global-fusion architecture is the \emph{formulation of the translation target}: direct absolute regression from pooled features fails (6.8\% ADD(-S), below what the depth median alone provides), while anchoring the prediction to a 3D point back-projected from the observed depth brings the same 36M-parameter model to 95.1\% ADD(-S) in a fully end-to-end evaluation with predicted detections --- above the per-pixel variant of DenseFusion under the same protocol and data regime, with no per-point fusion, no refinement, and no synthetic training data. A memorization analysis (retrieval and oracle nearest-neighbor bounds at 2.3\% and 7.1\%, and a flat accuracy profile up to $12^\circ$ of viewpoint novelty) supports the conclusion that the result reflects actual geometric generalization. In this regime, expressing the regression target relative to observed geometry appears to matter more than model capacity or input encoding.

\paragraph{Limitations and Future Work}
We identify four main limitations. \textbf{(i) Small objects}: the two smallest classes (ape, duck) remain the weakest (87--90\%), a consequence of the diameter-relative ADD threshold shrinking to 10--11\,mm; sub-pixel refinements of the crop or a local per-pixel branch could close this gap. \textbf{(ii) Extrapolation tail}: beyond $14^\circ$ from the nearest training view (2.4\% of the test set) accuracy drops to 67--72\%, marking the boundary of viewpoint interpolation; synthetic view augmentation~\cite{he2020pvn3d, he2021ffb6d} is the established remedy and is fully compatible with our formulation. \textbf{(iii) Single anchor}: the median anchor summarizes the visible surface with one point, which is robust to box jitter but discards the spatial structure that dense per-point methods exploit under heavy occlusion; a sparse multi-anchor extension is a natural middle ground. \textbf{(iv) Aspect-ratio distortion}: the square crop with side $\max(w,h)$ resized to $224{\times}224$ introduces non-isotropic resampling; square padding would preserve the aspect ratio at negligible cost.
